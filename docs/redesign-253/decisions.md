# Alternatives, recommendation and owner decisions

[designed: #253] **Recommend A: Country desk.** The game centers on a country's next challenge, with comparison-first Team decisions and a continuous Match centre journey. The ten annotated layouts show this direction. It preserves national context and original information density while giving each action a visible place and feedback. The recommendation is ready for a decision; it is not an approved redesign.

| Direction | Whole-game organization and visual approach | Strength / cost / deliberate tradeoff |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **A — Country desk (recommended)** | [designed] Five stable workspaces, persistent country/competition/next action, compact tables plus complete cards, newspaper-style news, schematic pitch and shared result schema. Paper/ink/emerald management surfaces flow into a darker broadcast match header. | [derived: original hub/card/table evidence; current assessment] Best fit for repeated information-rich decisions and national career continuity. Needs substantial page composition and missing-data contracts; a restrained visual language gives the pitch and consequences emphasis. |
| **B — Broadcast career** | [designed] A large cinematic next-match/news stage is the home; opponent, formation and hero player fill the main view. Team/competition details live in full-height drawers; live match becomes the primary destination. Same required parity data still exists in drawers. | [derived: compared to original squad/pool tasks] More immediate spectacle and pitch focus; player comparison and group fixtures need repeated drawer switches. Greater animation/art and adaptive-layout cost, plus additional access steps for dense management. Credible if the owner prioritizes watching over squad work. |
| **C — Modern command room** | [designed] Preserve original menu-like central choices and multi-panel cards, modernize typography/colors/desktop controls and add visible shortcuts. Squad/pool and match menus remain separate destinations. | [derived: original inventory; current assessment] Strong recognizable DOS rhythm and cheap conceptual mapping, but continued screen hopping and context-dependent actions work against the reported discovery problem. Density can become small text rather than useful comparison. |

[designed: #253] The preference is grounded in task frequency and decision proximity, not a popularity claim. A player repeatedly compares people, an opponent and campaign position before/after each match; A keeps those tasks adjacent. B remains a credible future presentation emphasis within A's Match centre, without imposing its drawer navigation on squad preparation. C supplies atmospheric details—bulletin, clear menu labels, team/role strips—rather than the whole interaction architecture. No additional unexplained mockup set is needed to choose among these plainly different organizations.

## Approval gate

[designed: owner #253] **Explicit owner direction approval is required before redesign implementation or any new child issue starts.** The owner should record the selected direction, permitted renderer/toolkit choice, first milestone and any named parity exceptions on #253 or its proposal PR. Completing this documentation issue, filing the backlog or obtaining an independent documentation approval is **not implementation approval**. No toolkit, backend-language or architecture migration is authorized. Every implementation still follows the repository's model-selection, worktree, task, independent review and exact-head merge process.

[designed: owner #253] Existing independently authorized work retains its own scope and authorization. The gate applies to this proposed redesign and its new children; it does not revoke T79/T84/T85 or any other independently authorized task. Proposed adaptations to those outcomes wait for selection and normal per-task authorization, without changing their issue bodies or active branches under this issue.
