# The game we want to finish

[designed: #266, for owner selection] **Manage a country, understand your players, live through its matches, and build its football story over successive campaigns.** GOAL2 should be an installable desktop national-team management game for people who enjoy choosing a side and following the consequences. Its central appeal is responsibility: these are your twenty players, your next opponent and your country's future. Complete information and meaningful supported choices matter as much as the pitch.

[designed: owner revision, 2026-10-20] **The original GOAL2 is the visual baseline.** The owner requested changes to the first vision: stronger functional colors, large characters, information directly in view, attribute bars, a maximum-size pitch, full commentary hidden by default with only recent lines visible, and retractable top/side bars. “In doubt, it should look like the original.” The former paper/ink/emerald recommendation is superseded. This is a desktop game, with the recognizable original screen language; a webpage/card-dashboard presentation is not the destination. [Recorded feedback and original-screen study](evidence.md#owner-correction-and-original-screen-study).

[designed] The [nineteen-screen visual walkthrough](storyboard.md) follows the same illustrative Holland career from Resume through a friendly, the next competitive fixture and later campaigns. Every new image is a **finished-target illustration**, including unavailable/error states. None is a current capture or executable prototype. [Current-build evidence](evidence.md#current-reality) stays separate. The revised candidate awaits owner review; requesting these changes did not approve the whole vision, commit the plan or authorize implementation.

[designed: owner, 2026-10-21] **Every screen the player sees follows this design**; that is the goal of the first release. **No attribute total is ever shown**: the original shows skill bars, characteristics and roles, never an overall ability. **Choosing a country lets the player browse its card and every player's complete card**, as the original's country selection does (Space: players).

[designed: owner, 2026-10-21] **Roles are one-letter codes**, as in the original (G S C b B D Ç m M F w W ¢ $), on the pitch, in lists and on cards. The accepted screens print two-letter roles; [role-codes/](role-codes/README.md) redraws the ten affected sheets and supersedes them for role display.

## Look and feel: unmistakably GOAL2

[designed: owner direction] Use black screens, large monospaced characters, thin functional rules and strong, varied color. Red identifies headings/field groups, gold attribute labels, green readiness/values and goalkeepers, cyan navigation/midfield, yellow emphasis/forwards and magenta help/events; defenders use red and selection has a silver field with dark text. Labels, positions and focus remain readable without color alone. This is a modern rendering of the original's information language, not a new simulation or copied original asset/font.

[designed] Information fills the working area: all twenty color-coded names beside a complete bar card, side-by-side outgoing/incoming comparisons, scorer columns and brightly labelled comparative statistics. Primary reading text is roughly 20–30px in the normal illustrations and 18px for the complete small-window card, rather than the first version's small card text. Preserve scalable text and contained scrolling/panes before reducing type. The existing seven-column compact table and 38/37-column expanded contracts remain available through a named Attribute table view; this visual proposal does not change their independently authorized delivery contracts or hide any field permanently.

[designed] All twelve skills and five characteristics use **segmented bars**, with the raw original unit beside the bar where supported. Known domains/formulas govern the display; no percentage, performance scale or invented descriptor replaces original meaning. The fictional cards fill descriptor slots with original vocabulary, explicitly labelled illustrative rather than claiming a decoded word-to-value lookup. Unsupported/unknown values show a clear unavailable explanation rather than a fabricated bar. Bar labels, values and the Player/Team instruction source remain available as accessible text.

[designed: owner direction] Top and side bars can each retract. A visible edge handle and keyboard action restore them; a focus shortcut may retract both. Retraction returns space to the active information without changing selection, draft, scroll position, fixture, job or engine state. Focus remains on the same control or returns to its opener, with an accessible announcement; do not automatically hide controls while someone edits or answers a prompt. The small window uses labelled Roster/Player/Tactics panes with large bar cards. Hiding chrome is presentation, not abandoning navigation or a draft.

## What a session feels like

[designed] On returning, you know which country you manage, where the campaign stands, who is next and what needs attention. The desk puts the next challenge beside the last result and squad readiness. A national bulletin and country records make the career feel larger than one fixture. Your country stays visible while you inspect another side; browsing never replaces your active career.

[designed] Team is a working surface: all twenty identities, the XI/bench split, a complete selected player bar card and your current plan. Compare an outgoing squad member with a national-pool candidate side by side, using the same field order. Keep national and club positions distinct; show all twelve original skills and five characteristics as bars, readiness, discipline and personal instructions. Compact/expanded table views retain the existing seven-column and 38-column squad/37-column pool contracts. Inspect, draft, review and confirm are distinct. An aggregate ability value never substitutes for the full player.

[designed] Preparation brings both named lineups and formations together with fixture type, venue, competition context and relevant stakes. It lets you review captain, roles and supported team/personal tactics before explicitly starting. Quick explains that it resolves without watched pitch playback and may still require supported decisions. Watch explains what can actually be controlled in this fixture. They do not promise identical outcomes. Slow/Normal/Fast is chosen before kickoff and remains fixed for the running match under the accepted playback contract.

[designed: owner direction] During play, **the pitch takes as much of the available window as possible**. Top/side navigation retracts; score and supplied clock/phase remain in a thin HUD, with only the last two commentary lines shown. Full commentary is hidden by default and opens on request, then gives its space back to the pitch when closed. [Default pitch view](storyboard.md#06-feel-the-match) and [the same instant with commentary/navigation opened](storyboard.md#15-open-commentary-only-when-wanted) make the tradeoff concrete. No permanent full-height commentary column competes with play.

[designed] Score, supplied clock/phase and commentary still tell the same story. Supported decisions announce themselves without forcing an always-visible coaching sidebar; an unavailable action explains why when opened. A supported halftime/injury choice brings up the complete relevant bar cards, allowance and explicit Resume. Extra time/shoot-outs are conditional phases, not routine extra screens after every draw. Actual opportunities, defaults and timing govern; retracting UI cannot dismiss a required prompt, answer it, pause the engine or create an intervention.

[designed] Results explain scorers, minutes, half/full/extra-time scores where applicable, penalty resolution, statistics and man of the match when supplied. Zero and Not recorded mean different things. Round results and Continue return to the changed country context. A friendly changes the recorded story and any supported player effects; it does not award qualification points. A competitive result changes only the supported competition state. Save, close and Resume return to the same committed career.

[designed] The complete career continues beyond the opening campaign: qualification, finals when earned, elimination, subsequent stages and cycles, age changes, retirement and replacement, national rankings and country records. Failure should still have a clear continuation. Actual progression, roster and manager consequences follow their evidenced contracts; a harsh bulletin does not itself mean dismissal. No new draw, ageing, retirement or replacement algorithm is proposed here.

## What “finished” means

[designed] The complete product can be accepted when a player can do the following in the installed game, with retained evidence rather than placeholders:

- Complete the session above and continue through the evidenced longer career, including qualifying success and failure, conditional match phases and later roster/cycle transitions.
- Find every evidenced original information category and decision at its useful moment, including less frequent country profiles, rankings/records, watch scheduling, friendly rearrangement, assistant selection, editable identities, local-manager handoffs and goal/summary retrospective functions. The [#253 field matrix](https://github.com/diegoami/goal2/blob/bd878531210478cba87143b7de95faf367f40e77/docs/redesign-253/information-parity.md) is the candidate detailed inventory to reconcile after selection, not a new backlog here.
- See actual retained/generated data and working actions for each accepted category. An unavailable label is honest interim behavior; it does not complete parity. Unknown original meanings remain named evidence gaps, never invented ratings or tooltips. A permanently reduced category needs a named owner exception.
- Reliably save, quit and resume without losing confirmed decisions; fail/cancel safely; receive understandable feedback for invalid selections, missing resources and unavailable routes. In-progress match saves remain excluded unless separately specified and proven.
- Read and operate the rich interface with mouse and keyboard at 1440×900 and 900×640 logical client sizes, at actual 1×/2× and increased text scale. Verify focus, table/card reading, screen-reader names/events and reduced motion on the selected supported platforms. These are acceptance targets, not a certification supplied by these pictures.
- Install, update and reopen supported desktop builds while preserving compatible saves or using an explicitly accepted migration. Platform/packaging claims need native evidence; proposed Windows/Linux coverage is not already accepted support. macOS remains an owner platform choice.

## A satisfying first delivery is smaller

[designed: recommendation, not a committed roadmap] The first release should let one manager resume/create a career with explicit identity limitations, inspect and confirm a complete Team/pool decision, prepare a supported friendly, choose Quick or view-only Watch, understand the result, reach the next fixture and Save/Resume. Competitive Quick can remain available within its accepted scope. It must make the repeated session coherent and readable, rather than merely reskinning separate pages. Complete cards depend on accepted T84 delivery; prominent entry and frozen speed depend on T85. This paragraph promises neither is present on main today.

[designed] First delivery must feel and operate as an installed desktop game. A coordinated native management/match window boundary remains a feasibility proposal, not an owner-selected concession; identity, focus, retractable controls, busy/cancel behavior and return to results need proof. A static composition does not prove embedding or justify a browser game. The exact window/toolkit decision follows the selected original-looking experience and #253's feasibility evidence; no migration is authorized here.

[designed] The first delivery is **not full parity**. It would explicitly leave additional watched modes/flags/quick continuation, friendly rearrangement, generated bulletins, full country records/rankings, durable goal/summary capture/export/replay and local-manager/assistant/identity capabilities unavailable until supported. Missing stages are explained boundaries, not fake future fixtures. Exact first-release boundaries must be selected below and reconciled with existing contracts; this vision adds no delivery tasks, dates or estimates.
