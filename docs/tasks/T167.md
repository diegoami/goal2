- **Scope**: *play next game* on the desk runs the original's match day in the new window: S14 → S15 → S16 → S17
  → S20 (half time) → S15 → S17 → S20 (full time) → S15 *exit* → the desk. This holds for a friendly and a
  qualifier. The task protects one claim: **the match day the player goes through is the original's order of
  screens, and every answer given on them is the answer the engine receives.**
  1. **S14, round results.** The round's other results arrive one by one, as the route resolves them before your
     match (`silent_until_managed`), and after it until the round ends. Your match is listed last, still to play.
     **Space** pauses, **←→** changes speed, **Enter** goes to your match.
  2. **S15, match menu.**
     - It shows the score, kind, location (home or away, from #562), the time (before, HALF TIME, FULL TIME,
       EXTRA TIME) and both formations and elevens.
     - The actions, with *play* highlighted first: each side's team tactics and players (read-only for the
       other side; yours open S10 and S08), tournament (S12), match stats (S20), **play** or **exit**.
     - **Ins** toggles automatic action replay; the flag is stored. The replay itself is T168's.
     - *play* names what follows: "choose your captain, then kick-off", or "second half".
     - **The watch question:** where the original asks *watch this match? (Y/N)* (E031 C35–C37), S15 asks it with
       Y/N. N plays a quick result: S17 with R pressed (decision 3).
  3. **S16, choose a player.**
     - **The captain before kick-off.** The headline is "Choose your captain to kick off". The highlight starts on
       the computer's pick (T143's rule). ↑↓, **Enter** chooses, **C** takes the computer's pick.
     - **The set-piece taker during play.** The engine's taker decision is passed over the bridge and the pipe as
       a new decision context, and S16 asks it with the original's wait: the computer's pick stands after the
       timeout (6–12 s in T156-R1; cite the finding for the exact rule, or name it `[assumed]`).
     - **The shoot-out order.** The `shootout_order` decision, picked one by one; **Esc** undoes a pick.
  4. **S20, match board.**
     - At half time and full time, the statistics the bridge's outcome and events give: score and half-time score,
       scorers and minutes, and the counts the design lists.
     - A count the route does not have says so; nothing is invented.
     - After a quick result, the QUICK RESULT banner instead.
     - **Enter** continues to S15, and **Esc** opens S19 (until T180, S19 is the default answer with a note).
  5. **Half time and extra time** go S17 → S20 → S15 (*play* continues) → S17. Substitution offers and other stops
     keep T166's defaults with a note until T180 builds S18 and S19.
  6. **One bridge for the window's life.** The match-day controller keeps one bridge on the window, so all the
     window's matches play in one engine process (D3). T166's per-match bridge goes.
  7. **#562, the home side.** Find which fixture side the original treats as home (F023's schedule, cited), expose
     it on the model, and use it on S15, the desk (*friendly away*) and S17's colours (home light red).
  8. After full time and *exit*, the round's remaining results show on S14. The result is published once through
     the bridge, and the player returns to the desk. A qualifier publishes through the competitive publication
     (T176).

- **Hazards**:
  - **Splitting the engine's answers.** Each screen's answer is sent once, through the bridge, in the order the
    engine asked. Never answer a decision the screen has not shown.
  - **Inventing statistics.** S20 shows only counts the route produces.
  - **Size.** If this outgrows about two hours, stop after S14–S16 with the flow working, and report. The main
    session splits the rest.
  - **Machine load.** Run your modules one at a time, never the whole suite.
